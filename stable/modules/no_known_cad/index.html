Low-risk recommendations

Stable low-risk chest pain generally does not require routine diagnostic testing.

Primary Test
No testing recommendedA
Why?
  • Low-risk stable chest pain has a low likelihood of obstructive CAD.
  • Routine testing may increase false positives and downstream testing.
  • Contemporary pretest probability models can help confirm low-risk status.
How?

No diagnostic cardiac test is ordered initially. The clinician focuses on clinical follow-up, risk-factor assessment, and reassessment if symptoms change.

Evidence?
  • 2021 ACC/AHA Chest Pain Guideline: no testing recommended for low-risk stable chest pain.
  • Contemporary pretest probability models reduce overestimation of obstructive CAD.
Acceptable Alternative
CAC or Exercise ECG in selected casesB
Why?
  • CAC may help refine risk when uncertainty remains.
  • Exercise ECG may be reasonable if the patient can exercise and ECG is interpretable.
  • This is a selected-case strategy, not routine testing for all low-risk patients.
How?

CAC uses noncontrast CT to quantify coronary calcification. Exercise ECG evaluates symptoms, exercise capacity, hemodynamics, and ECG changes during exercise.

Evidence?
  • 2021 ACC/AHA Chest Pain Guideline: CAC or exercise ECG may be considered in selected low-risk patients.
  • Exercise ECG requires adequate exercise capacity and interpretable baseline ECG.

Stable chest pain

This module is intended for stable chest pain evaluation. Use acute pathways for suspected ACS or unstable features.

Red flags / exclusions

  • Concern for ACS, hemodynamic instability, or high-risk ECG changes
  • Severe respiratory distress or severe hypotension
  • Use local institutional protocols
