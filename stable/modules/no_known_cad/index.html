Choose one guideline-supported strategy.

Primary Test

No testing recommendedA

Why?
  • Low-risk stable chest pain has low likelihood of obstructive CAD.
  • Routine testing may increase false positives and downstream testing.
  • Contemporary pretest probability models can help confirm low-risk status.
How?

No diagnostic cardiac test is ordered initially. The clinician focuses on follow-up, risk-factor assessment, and reassessment if symptoms change.

Evidence?
  • 2021 ACC/AHA Chest Pain Guideline: no testing recommended for low-risk stable chest pain.
  • Contemporary pretest probability models reduce overestimation of obstructive CAD.

Acceptable Alternative

CAC or Exercise ECG in selected casesB

Why?
  • CAC may refine risk when uncertainty remains.
  • Exercise ECG may be reasonable if the patient can exercise and ECG is interpretable.
  • This is selected-case testing, not routine testing for all low-risk patients.
How?

CAC uses noncontrast CT to quantify coronary calcification. Exercise ECG evaluates symptoms, functional capacity, hemodynamics, and ECG changes during exercise.

Evidence?
  • 2021 ACC/AHA Chest Pain Guideline: CAC or exercise ECG may be considered in selected low-risk patients.
  • Exercise ECG requires adequate exercise capacity and interpretable baseline ECG.
